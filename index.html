Cold Call One-Sheet

Vern · Director of Special Projects · Robertson Bright Inc.
MISSION: BOOK A 20–30 MINUTE WORKFLOW AUDIT

1 The opener · 20–30 seconds

“Hey, Vern? Hey, it’s Deep. I’ll keep this brief because I know you’re probably in the middle of a project.

I work with businesses to reduce manual work and streamline operations using practical AI, especially repetitive data entry, job tracking, and field-to-office reporting.

I was looking at the kind of complex projects RBI handles and thought it was worth connecting.

“Would you be open to a quick 20 to 30-minute call next week to see if there are workflows we could simplify and save your team time?”

🛑 STOP TALKING. LET HIM RESPOND.Don’t fill the silence. The goal is a conversation, not a full presentation.

2 Objection quick replies

“What do you actually do?”
“We look for places where project teams spend too much time copying information between site reports, spreadsheets, emails, and project tools. We automate those handoffs so people spend less time on admin.”
Ask: “How are you handling those handoffs today?”
“We already have software / an ERP.”
“Absolutely. I’m not looking to replace your systems. I’m interested in the manual work that still happens between them.”
Ask: “How do site updates get into your central tracking today?”
“Just send me an email.”
“Of course. So I can make it relevant, is there a workflow you’d want me to focus on, like site reporting, change orders, or material tracking?”
If engaged: “Would a quick 15-minute walkthrough next week be useful?”
“We’re too busy.”
“I understand. Would it make more sense to schedule something next week when you have a little more breathing room?”
“Not interested. Everyone is pitching AI.”
“Fair enough. I’m not calling to sell AI for its own sake. I’m interested in whether there’s a real operational bottleneck worth fixing. If things are running smoothly, there may be nothing to do.”
“How much does it cost?”
“It depends on the workflow and the potential return. I’d first want to understand your process and whether there’s enough value to justify automating it.”

3 If he shows interest · discover one pain

Pick one question. Listen to the answer. Follow up naturally.
ADMIN & PAPERWORK“Where do your project managers lose the most time on paperwork or admin?”
FIELD-TO-OFFICE REPORTING“How do site updates make their way into your central tracking systems today?”
CHANGE ORDERS“How do you track scope changes and make sure they’re reflected in your project records?”
COORDINATION“Are teams chasing updates across emails, texts, and spreadsheets, or is that handoff already automated?”

4 Close · book the meeting

“That’s interesting. I’d love to understand that workflow a little better and see if there’s a practical way to simplify it.”

“How about a 20-minute call next week? I can come prepared with a few ideas specific to your process. Would Tuesday or Thursday afternoon work better?”

When he agrees: “Perfect. What’s the best email for the invite? I’ll send a short agenda so you know what we’ll cover.”

5 Keep the strategy simple

  1. Lead with operations, not AI. Talk about manual work, delays, and handoffs.
  2. Don’t assume the pain. Ask and let him describe the current process.
  3. Keep it conversational. No long tech explanation or unsupported savings claims.
  4. Sell the next conversation. Don’t try to close a project on a cold call.
